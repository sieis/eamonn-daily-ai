keyDetails:
  - "Released October 13, based on a survey of 1,416 U.S. enterprise leaders and entry- and mid-level employees"
  - "80% of managers and above say their department's AI investments are yielding positive returns, but only 28% call those returns strongly positive"
  - "Departments tracking at least one growth measure, such as revenue or customer results, were far more likely to report strong returns (35%) than departments tracking efficiency alone (13%)"
  - "These are self-reported answers and show correlation, not proof that tracking growth causes better results. The study's definition of 'growth' is also fairly broad"
  - "A separate Komprise survey out October 15 found a similar gap: 68% of organizations call themselves AI-mature, but only 28% have measured the return"
whyItMatters: "Many businesses have bought AI tools and feel good about them, but can't say what the tools are actually worth. The pattern in this study is that people who set a real goal and count the results get more out of AI than those who just count hours saved."
consultingAngle: "Before you roll out or renew an AI tool, pick one number it should move, such as quotes sent per week, response time to leads, or invoices processed per hour, and record where you are today. Check it again after 60 to 90 days. Saving time is only half the story if you don't know what the time was spent on afterward. This is also an easy way to decide which subscriptions to keep and which to cancel."
