keyDetails:
  - "OpenClaw Enterprise (OCE) is a free, open-source 'control plane,' a management layer that decides what AI agents can access and records what they do"
  - "It began as an internal OpenAI project, was donated to the OpenClaw Foundation, and was developed further with Red Hat and Nvidia"
  - "Features include separate workspaces for different teams, fine-grained permissions, sandboxing, tamper-evident audit logs, and a default of denying tool access unless it's explicitly allowed"
  - "It can be self-hosted today using Docker Compose or Kubernetes; the Foundation says it's for internal pilots now, with a 1.0 release planned later this year"
  - "The Foundation says it will stay free for any organization to use"
whyItMatters: "As AI agents start doing real work, from answering email to touching company data, someone has to control what they're allowed to do. Think of this as the rulebook and security camera for AI workers. It's notable because competitors are backing a shared, free standard instead of each locking you into their own."
consultingAngle: "This is aimed at companies with an IT team, so most small businesses won't install it directly. The takeaway for everyone is the checklist it represents: agents should have limited permissions, run in a contained environment, and leave an audit trail. Ask any AI vendor you work with whether they offer those three things. If you're a mid-size or larger company planning to run many agents, have your IT lead evaluate OCE as a pilot, since a free open option may beat paying for a proprietary governance product."
