Participating businesses also get three months of ChatGPT Business at no cost. The pilot is live in 20 communities, from New York and Chicago to Fargo and Jackson, Mississippi."

keyDetails:
  - "What it is: a network of 'OpenWorks Partners', local individuals and small consulting teams who give small businesses free, one-on-one help using AI on a real problem the owner chooses, such as reaching new customers, running an ad campaign or building a tool the team keeps using"
  - "What businesses get: free hands-on support with tools they already use, three months of ChatGPT Business at no cost, and no technical experience required. OpenAI hasn't said what happens after the three free months"
  - "Who qualifies: independently owned U.S. small businesses like restaurants, retailers, service firms and manufacturers. Software/SaaS companies, venture-backed startups and private-equity-owned businesses are excluded"
  - "Where: the pilot covers 20 communities including Chicago, Kansas City, Fargo, Madison, Cleveland, South Bend, St. Louis, Jackson, Charlotte, Oklahoma City, Charleston, Nashville, Austin, Houston, Atlanta, Los Angeles, Incline Village, Olympia, Seattle and New York"
  - "What partners do: spend days each month working in person with local businesses and host a monthly community workshop, in return for a stipend of up to $8,000 a month plus training and resources"
  - "Early examples (reported by the businesses, not audited): a Fargo gift shop built an inventory dashboard it estimated would otherwise cost about $50,000, and a Charleston microgreens grower said ChatGPT-written pitches lifted his restaurant response rate from 20% to 75%"
  - "How to sign up: separate forms on OpenAI's site for businesses that want help, people who want to become partners, and nominations"
whyItMatters: "Most small businesses have heard plenty about AI but have never had someone sit down with them and show them how to use it on their own work. OpenWorks is OpenAI putting real people, and real money, behind closing that gap. If you run a local business in one of these cities, you can get free help with a specific problem, which is the fastest way to find out whether AI is worth your time."
consultingAngle: "If you're in a pilot city, apply with one clear, measurable problem rather than 'help us with AI': for example, cutting the hours spent on weekly inventory counts, or answering quote requests faster. Before the partner shows up, write down how long that task takes today so you can measure the change. And plan for month four: three free months of ChatGPT Business is a trial, so decide up front what result would make it worth paying for, and make sure anything the partner builds is something your team understands and can keep running on its own."
