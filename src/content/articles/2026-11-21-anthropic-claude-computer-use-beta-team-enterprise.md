keyDetails:
  - "What it is: Claude can look at your screen and click, type and move between the desktop apps you approve, so it can do tasks in software that has no direct connection to Claude"
  - "Where it works: the Claude Desktop app on Mac (macOS 14 or later) and Windows. You can also start tasks from Claude on the web or your phone, as long as Claude Desktop is open on the computer it will use"
  - "Off by default: only an Owner or Primary Owner can turn it on for the organization, and each person then has to turn it on in their own Claude Desktop settings and approve each app the first time Claude asks"
  - "App controls: owners can keep a block list (for example, your password manager) or an allow list (only these apps). Enterprise plans can limit it to certain roles; on Team plans the setting applies to everyone"
  - "Anthropic's own warnings: there is no sandbox between Claude and your apps, misleading content on a web page or in an email could trick it into the wrong action, and it can change or delete files outside the folders you've connected"
  - "Screenshots Claude takes are saved with the chat and may contain confidential information. Organizations with HIPAA-ready setups, customer-managed encryption keys or school (FERPA/K-12) settings can't turn it on yet"
  - "It is rolling out to organizations gradually, so it may not appear in your account yet"
whyItMatters: "Most AI assistants can only work with apps that have been specifically connected to them. Computer use removes that limit: Claude operates your computer the way a person would, by looking at the screen and clicking. That means it can help with older or niche software that will never get an AI integration. It also means the AI is acting directly on your machine, so a mistake affects your real files and accounts, not a copy."
consultingAngle: "Look at this as a way to handle the tedious copy-paste work between systems that don't talk to each other, like moving order details from an email into an old inventory program or filling the same customer info into three portals. Don't turn it on for the whole company on day one. Start with one or two people, use an allow list of the specific apps involved, and block anything holding passwords, payroll or banking. Write down which tasks it is approved for and have someone check the results for the first few weeks. Google (Gemini for Mac), OpenAI (dots) and Microsoft (Copilot) are all launching similar features, so having a short internal policy on 'AI that controls a computer' now will save you from re-deciding this for every tool."
