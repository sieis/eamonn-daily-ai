summary: "Google is rolling out skills in Workspace: saved instructions, templates and reference files that Gemini follows in Gmail, Docs, Slides, Drive and Chat. Separately, Gemini's email summaries and 'Help me write' are expanding to all Workspace users 18 and older, switched on by default."

keyDetails:
  - "A skill is a reusable set of instructions, such as your brand voice, a proposal template or a contract checklist, plus any Drive files it should reference. Gemini applies it automatically when relevant, or you call it with an @mention"
  - "Google's examples: keeping brand voice consistent across Gmail drafts, proposals and decks; checking contracts, proposals or invoices against guidelines stored in Drive; and turning meeting notes and chats into recurring status updates"
  - "Available on Business Starter, Standard and Plus, and Enterprise Starter, Standard and Plus. Rollout began November 9 for Rapid Release domains and starts November 30 for Scheduled Release domains, with up to 15 days for features to appear"
  - "Skills are on by default. Admins can switch them off for the whole company, a department or a group, and audit skill activity in logs"
  - "Limits: English only, not yet in Google Sheets or Meet, created on the web only, and they do not sync with Gems in the separate Gemini app. Sharing a skill gives the other person a copy, and they still need access to any Drive files it uses"
  - "Separately, from November 10 Gemini in Gmail's thread summaries and 'Help me write' are expanding to all Workspace customers and personal Google accounts 18 and older with smart features on. They are on by default; users can turn them off and admins can disable them"
whyItMatters: "The biggest complaint about AI writing tools is that everything they produce sounds generic. Skills let a business write down once how it wants things done, the tone, the template, the checklist, and have the AI follow that every time, for every employee. And because Gemini in Gmail is now switched on for almost everyone, many teams will start using AI in email this month whether or not anyone planned it."
consultingAngle: "If your company runs on Google Workspace, this is a quick win and a policy question at the same time. The quick win: pick two or three documents your team rewrites constantly, such as quotes, follow-up emails or weekly updates, and turn your best example plus your rules into a shared skill. New hires get the house style on day one. The policy question: both skills and Gemini in Gmail arrive switched on. Decide now whether that is what you want, tell staff what they may and may not paste into AI, and have your admin check the settings before the November 30 rollout reaches Scheduled Release accounts. A short written AI policy covers most of it."
