keyDetails:
  - "The system, called array-associated reverse transcriptases (ART), was found in bacteriophages (viruses that infect bacteria) and pairs a reverse transcriptase enzyme with a partner gene sitting next to DNA repeats arranged the way a CRISPR array is"
  - "Anthropic set about 950 Claude agents loose on a database of over 200,000 known reverse transcriptases; over 21 hours and 210 million tokens, they narrowed the field to 3,500 candidate systems and then 20 stand-outs, with one agent flagging the never-before-characterized ART pattern"
  - "This came out of Anthropic's Life Sciences lab, a Bay Area research group launched this spring, where human scientists specializing in computational biology and protein work review and validate whatever the AI turns up rather than the AI running the wet-lab work itself"
  - "CRISPR pioneer Feng Zhang, of MIT and the Broad Institute, called it \"an exciting example of how AI agents can contribute to biological discovery,\" though Anthropic is upfront that ART's actual biological function is still unknown, the finding hasn't been peer-reviewed, and some outside scientists have been more skeptical of how significant it really is"
