keyDetails:
  - "Zensurance surveyed 1,000 Canadian small business owners, entrepreneurs, and self-employed professionals in a national Pollfish poll fielded June 5-22, 2026, then published the results September 14"
  - "32% of respondents say AI has had little or no impact on their business so far, and 27% say they aren't sure how it would even apply to what they do — nearly six in ten either seeing nothing or not knowing where to start"
  - "Only about 9% credit AI with a meaningful gain in productivity or cost savings, and 7% say adopting it actually hurt their revenue, so even among those who have tried it, a clean win is still the exception, not the rule"
  - "This comes against a backdrop of already-strained small business sentiment — a separate Zensurance/BDC confidence index found small business confidence at a three-year low, with owners citing rising costs and shrinking cash reserves as bigger day-to-day concerns than technology adoption"
  - "It's a notable contrast with other recent surveys this month showing steadily climbing AI adoption among small businesses elsewhere — a reminder that national and industry-specific numbers can tell very different stories depending on who's asked and how"
whyItMatters: "It's easy to read the AI news cycle and assume every business but yours has already figured this out and is pulling ahead. This survey says otherwise: a large share of real small business owners haven't seen a payoff yet, don't know where they'd even start, or tried it and it didn't help. That doesn't mean AI isn't worth exploring — it means you're not as far behind as the headlines make it feel."
consultingAngle: "The businesses in this survey who saw no benefit almost certainly skipped straight to 'we should use AI' without picking a specific, boring, repetitive task to point it at first — the same mistake that shows up across nearly every adoption report this year. Before spending more time or money on AI tools, pick one concrete recurring task (drafting the same type of email, summarizing the same type of call, sorting the same type of request) and measure whether it actually saves time on that one thing before expanding further."
