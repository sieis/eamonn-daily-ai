title: "Apple Launches a Research App to Study How People Use AI for Health Questions"
date: 2026-11-16
summary: "Apple released the Health AI Usage Study app on November 15, built with medical AI company OpenEvidence. Up to 200,000 U.S. adults can ask health questions, get answers drawn from medical journals and rate how useful they were. Apple has not said whether the feature will come to Siri or the Health app."
source: 'https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/15/apple-launches-new-app-to-study-how-people-use-ai-for-health/'

formatFit: 'Short-form'

keyDetails:
  - "Available to download in the U.S. from November 15. Apple wants up to 200,000 participants over more than 12 months"
  - "Who can join: U.S. residents over 21 with an iPhone 15 or later, iOS 26 or later and Apple Intelligence turned on"
  - "How it works: you ask a health question and get an answer based on clinical sources including The New England Journal of Medicine, JAMA and Nature, simplified for everyday use. You rate the answer and fill in a monthly survey"
  - "Optional: you can share Apple Health data such as resting heart rate, sleep, activity or lab results for more personal suggestions. The app can also help you prepare for a doctor's visit and point you to a provider for anything concerning"
  - "Privacy: Apple says data is anonymized before it is sent to a secure outside cloud provider, will not be used to train AI models, and that OpenEvidence deletes questions and answers after 24 hours"
  - "This is a study, not a product launch. TechCrunch notes it is unclear whether Apple will add AI health answers to the Health app or Siri"
  - "Context: OpenAI said earlier this year that people ask ChatGPT more than 300 million health questions a week"
whyItMatters: "People already ask chatbots about symptoms, medications and test results, often with no idea where the answer came from. Apple is taking the slow route: answers tied to medical journals, a large study and feedback from real users before deciding what to ship. If you are curious, joining is a low-risk way to try a more careful version of AI health advice. It is still not a substitute for a doctor."
consultingAngle: "Two takeaways for business owners. First, your employees are almost certainly asking AI health and benefits questions already. If you offer health coverage or wellness programs, point staff to trustworthy sources, and keep AI tools away from decisions about sick leave, accommodations or insurance. Second, notice how Apple is rolling this out: a limited trial, answers grounded in named sources, a rating on every response and a clear promise about what happens to the data. That is a good template for bringing AI into any sensitive part of your own business, such as customer advice, finance or HR. Start small, tie answers to documents you trust, collect feedback, and write down your data rules before you expand."
