keyDetails:
  - "Who: the NGA's bipartisan Governors Council to Secure American AI Dominance, led by Govs. Wes Moore (D-MD), Mike Braun (R-IN), Josh Stein (D-NC) and Spencer Cox (R-UT)"
  - "The split they propose: the federal government handles national and international risks while AI models are being developed. States keep control over how AI is used once it's deployed, in areas like consumer protection, kids, workers and discrimination"
  - "Six principles: protect innovation and the public, support workers through job changes, set clear federal and state roles, match safeguards to the level of risk, cut duplicate or conflicting rules, and avoid policies that favor large companies or create barriers for smaller firms"
  - "Related coverage reports the governors also want independent safety reviews and incident reporting for the most advanced models"
  - "Moore: 'We agree that humans must stay in control, and we must protect our people.' Cox: 'We don't have to choose between innovation and safety'"
  - "These are recommendations to the incoming Congress. No bill or sponsor has been announced"
whyItMatters: "Right now a business using AI may face different rules in every state it sells into. The governors are signaling that both parties want fewer conflicting rules for the companies building AI models, but that states won't give up their say over how AI treats customers and employees. Expect state rules on AI in hiring, customer service and consumer protection to keep coming."
consultingAngle: "Don't wait for Washington to settle this. The rules most likely to affect a small or mid-sized business are the state-level ones about how you use AI: screening job applicants, talking to customers, making decisions about people. Keep a simple list of where your business uses AI and which states your customers and employees are in, tell people when they're dealing with AI, and keep a person in the loop on decisions about hiring, pricing or credit. Those habits line up with almost every proposal on the table, federal or state, and make whatever comes next much easier to comply with."
