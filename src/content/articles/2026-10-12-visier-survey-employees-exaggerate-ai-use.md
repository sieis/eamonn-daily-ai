keyDetails:
  - "Visier, a workforce analytics company, surveyed 1,000 US full-time employees and found 48% have exaggerated their AI usage or expertise to colleagues or leadership. Visier calls this 'performative AI'"
  - "45% say they feel pressure to use AI at work even when they are not confident they are using it well"
  - "Only 28% of employees believe their leaders fully understand how staff actually use AI"
  - "Visier links the behavior to employer mandates and comparisons of AI-driven productivity between coworkers"
  - "Caveat: this is a vendor-run, self-reported survey, so it shows how people say they behave, not a measured result. It is also separate from earlier research (for example, a KPMG and University of Melbourne study) showing many workers hide their AI use, which is the opposite problem, so do not mix the numbers"
whyItMatters: "Companies are pushing staff to use AI, and many report adoption numbers to boards and investors. If a large share of people are inflating those numbers to look good, the reports are misleading and leaders may be paying for tools nobody uses well. It also suggests many workers want training and are afraid to ask for it."
consultingAngle: "If you track 'AI adoption' by counting logins or asking people in a survey, you may be measuring who feels safe claiming to use it. Better signals are specific: which tasks got faster, how many hours were saved, and what the error rate is. Make it safe to say 'I don't know how to do this yet' by offering short, practical training tied to real jobs, and share working examples from your own team rather than setting a generic mandate. Start with one process, such as drafting customer replies or summarizing meetings, and measure it before and after."
