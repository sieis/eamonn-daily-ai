  - "Who answered: 156 people who work on AI at organizations with at least 100 employees, or who consult for them. The survey ran in October and was published November 10"
  - "38% said their organization delayed, scaled back or canceled an AI project in the past 12 months because it cost more than expected"
  - "Only 12% blamed a shortage of computing capacity for the same outcome"
  - "79% expect their AI budget to grow over the next year anyway"
  - "26% said AI spending is judged by a measurable financial return that could be defended to finance. About 20% said finance had actually reviewed and signed off on a return figure"

whyItMatters: "Headlines about AI often focus on chip shortages and giant data centers. For the companies actually trying to use AI, the more common problem is simpler: the bill came in higher than planned. Budgets keep rising even though most firms cannot yet show what the money returned."
consultingAngle: "The lesson scales down to any business paying for AI. Before you start a project, write down what it is expected to save or earn and how you will check. Hours saved per week, tickets closed without staff, or invoices sent faster are all fine measures. Then watch for the costs that surprise people: pricing that charges by usage, per-seat licenses for staff who rarely log in, and the time spent on setup and review. Start with one workflow, put a monthly spending cap on it, and review after 60 days. A small project with a number attached is easier to defend, and easier to expand, than a large one that runs on faith."
