title: "Etsy Says It Will Rewrite Its AI Training Terms After Longtime Sellers Close Their Shops"
date: 2026-11-21
summary: "Etsy's new terms, taking effect December 16, let Etsy and its 'third party partners and vendors' use sellers' listing photos, descriptions and reviews to train AI, with no clear opt-out for U.S. sellers. After well-known sellers shut down shops in protest, Etsy told The New York Times on November 20 it will revise the terms and send sellers a clarified version soon."
source: 'https://www.shopifreaks.com/etsy-says-it-will-revise-its-new-terms-after-longtime-sellers-quit-over-a-clause-letting-its-partners-train-ai-on-their-work/'

audience: 'Solo/Creator'
formatFit: 'Both'

keyDetails:
  - "What changed: Etsy published new Terms of Use on October 26 that take effect December 16. They add explicit permission to 'use Your Content to develop, train, test and improve artificial intelligence and machine learning technologies'"
  - "What's covered: listing photos, descriptions, product reviews and forum posts. The license Etsy gets is perpetual and irrevocable, so deleting a listing or closing a shop may not end it"
  - "No easy opt-out in the U.S.: there's no account setting. Reporters found the only route for U.S. sellers is contacting Etsy's data protection officer. EU and UK sellers may have more rights to object"
  - "Etsy's position: VP of customer operations James Ossman says Etsy won't train AI designed to copy sellers' work or replace their creativity, and that the content is used for fraud detection, search and recommendations, and AI seller tools. That promise isn't in the terms themselves"
  - "The backlash: illustrator Aaron Millard deleted a shop he ran for nearly a decade, and stationery brand Cognitive Surplus closed its 13-year-old store. How many sellers have left is unknown"
  - "Meanwhile, the same terms bar outside companies from training AI on Etsy data without permission, and let Etsy restrict AI shopping agents, which may have to identify who they're acting for"
  - "The revised wording hasn't been published yet"
whyItMatters: "Any business that sells through someone else's platform, whether that's Etsy, Amazon, a marketplace app or social media, is agreeing to that platform's terms about how your photos and words can be used. AI has made those terms matter a lot more. Etsy's reversal shows that sellers pushing back together can change a policy, but only if they notice the change before it takes effect."
consultingAngle: "If you sell on Etsy, watch for the revised terms before December 16 and read the AI section yourself rather than relying on summaries. Save copies of your original photos and descriptions somewhere you control. This is also a good moment to review every platform you depend on: search each one's terms for 'machine learning' or 'artificial intelligence' and note what you've agreed to. Creative and handmade businesses especially should consider building a direct channel, like an email list or their own Shopify or Squarespace store, so a single policy change can't decide the future of the business."
