keyDetails:
  - "Who was asked: 1,000 IT decision-makers at large companies in the U.S., UK and Germany, surveyed July 21 to August 4, 2026, by research firm 3GEM for SnapLogic"
  - "54% have abandoned AI projects because the business value was unclear"
  - "48% struggle, or expect to struggle, to measure the return on AI agents. 50% feel pressure to keep investing even when value is hard to prove"
  - "89% have overspent on AI agents, and 64% call the overspending moderate or significant. Only 42% can accurately forecast future AI costs"
  - "Confidence is high anyway: 85% say they are deploying agents at scale or are ready to, and 90% trust, or would trust, the work agents produce"
  - "36% say their AI spending is outpacing their ability to manage and govern the agents"
  - "77% say their agents have, or would have, full or extensive access to company data and systems"
  - "Keep in mind: SnapLogic sells software that connects AI to business systems, and all figures are self-reported"
whyItMatters: "AI agents are programs that carry out multi-step tasks on their own, like processing an invoice or answering a support ticket from start to finish. Big companies have rushed to try them. This survey suggests many started without a clear idea of what success would look like, so when costs climbed, they could not show the project was worth it. It lines up with other recent studies showing AI projects stalling over cost overruns."
consultingAngle: "The lesson for any business, big or small: decide how you will measure an AI project before you start it. Write down one number it should move, such as hours spent on invoices each week, response time on support tickets, or cost per order, and record where that number is today. Set a monthly budget cap with your AI vendor so costs cannot run away. Start with one narrow job and a 60- to 90-day check-in, and stop or change course if the number has not moved. The 77% figure deserves attention too: give an agent access only to the systems it needs for its one job. Projects with a clear target, a budget and limited access are the ones that survive."
