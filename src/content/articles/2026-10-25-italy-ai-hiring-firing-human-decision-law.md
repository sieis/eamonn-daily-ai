keyDetails:
  - "Legislative Decree No. 179 of October 7, 2026 was published October 8 and took effect October 23. It brings Italian law in line with the EU AI Act"
  - "Decisions to set up, change or end an employment relationship, including discipline and performance evaluations, cannot rest solely on automated processing. Worker consent does not change that"
  - "Reported consequence: a dismissal made in breach of the rule is void"
  - "Employers must inform workers before AI processing begins, and workers can ask a human for a clear explanation of a decision that affects them"
  - "Commentators disagree on whether candidate screening is covered. One analysis says searching for and selecting candidates is outside the rule, while an earlier draft appeared to include recruitment"
  - "Caveat: one law firm noted the article text was not yet on Italy's official legal sites as of October 19, so much of the coverage relies on secondary summaries. Check the final text with local counsel"
whyItMatters: "More and more software now scores employees, flags performance problems and ranks job applicants. Italy is saying that a computer's score can inform a decision about a person's job, but it cannot be the whole decision. Other countries are heading in a similar direction, so this is an early look at the rules many employers may face."
consultingAngle: "If you use AI to screen applicants, rate performance or schedule staff, write down where a person reviews and signs off before anything affecting someone's job happens. Keep a short record of who reviewed what and why. If you employ people in Italy or elsewhere in the EU, ask your software vendors how their tools support human review and explanations, and get that answer in writing. Even outside the EU, this is simply good practice and protects you if a decision is challenged."
