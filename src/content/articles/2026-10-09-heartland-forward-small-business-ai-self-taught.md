keyDetails:
  - "Heartland Forward and Echelon Insights surveyed 691 small business owners online between September 3 and 11, 2026, including 308 in heartland states. It is a non-probability sample, so treat the numbers as a snapshot, not a census"
  - "43% of small businesses currently use AI. 70% of those say the effect on their finances has been net positive, and 93% say it saves them time"
  - "83% of owners who use AI taught themselves by experimenting; only 12% learned through a class or training program"
  - "Among owners who do not use AI, 52% say they have not seen a need for it"
  - "Different surveys give different headline numbers because 'using AI' is defined differently. A Small Business and Entrepreneurship Council survey (Sept 11-19, 516 owners) found 58% of small employers use AI agents regularly or occasionally, while a Goldman Sachs survey found 76% use AI but only 14% have it embedded in core operations"
whyItMatters: "If you have wondered whether you are behind on AI, this is a useful reality check. Fewer than half of small businesses in this survey use it, most of those who do figured it out on their own, and the large majority say it saves them time. The gap between 'tried it' and 'built it into how we work' is where most businesses still are."
consultingAngle: "Two takeaways for an owner. First, self-teaching works for getting started but it tends to produce scattered, individual habits rather than shared processes. Pick one repeatable task, such as answering common customer questions or summarizing meeting notes, write down how the team should do it, and measure the time saved over a month. Second, when you read an AI adoption statistic, check how 'use' was defined. A figure that counts anyone who has ever opened a chatbot is very different from one that counts AI built into daily operations. Free training exists too: America's SBDC recently announced OpenAI-backed AI training for small businesses."
