summary: "Google DeepMind released EmbeddingGemma 2 on October 6, a free, open model that turns text, images, audio and video into a form computers can search by meaning. It is small enough to run on a phone or laptop, so your files never have to leave the device. Performance claims come from Google and have not been independently tested."

keyDetails:
  - "What it is: an 'embedding' model. It does not chat or write. It converts documents, photos, audio and video into numbers that capture meaning, so software can find 'the contract about the Denver lease' even if those exact words never appear"
  - "EmbeddingGemma 2 handles text, code, images, audio and video in one shared space, so one search can find a matching photo, voice memo or PDF. Google lists it at 740 million parameters, which is small by AI standards"
  - "It is released under the Apache 2.0 license, which generally allows commercial use, with downloads on Hugging Face and Kaggle"
  - "Google says the full model runs in roughly 567 MB of memory (the text-only part needs about 191 MB), which is within reach of a recent phone or ordinary laptop"
  - "It works with common tools including Ollama, LM Studio, llama.cpp and sentence-transformers, so a technical person can try it without a large setup"
  - "Caveat: we found no independent benchmark results yet, so the quality claims rest on Google's own announcement. Coverage is also from secondary sources, so check Google's page before relying on specifics"
whyItMatters: "Many AI tools that search your documents, emails or photos send that material to a cloud service first. A model this small can do the 'find things by meaning' part on your own device, which is cheaper and keeps sensitive files in-house. It is a building block, not an app you download and use, but it is the kind of piece that makes private, low-cost search tools possible."
