On iPhone the switch is literally labeled 'Block Siri AI.' Signal admits the fix is 'incomplete' and is asking Apple and Google for a proper way for apps to opt out of AI access."

keyDetails:
  - "What changed: Signal now restricts screenshots by default on iPhone and Android so AI agents built into the phone can't read what's on the Signal screen"
  - "On iPhone: the setting is called 'Block Siri AI' and targets the agent features built into iOS 27"
  - "On Android: it's called 'Screen security' and works on versions where Signal can detect an AI assistant trying to read the screen"
  - "You can switch it off under Settings > Privacy > App Security, but Signal warns that doing so may expose your messages to AI agents"
  - "Signal calls the protection 'incomplete': it doesn't stop agents from reaching files already stored on the device"
  - "Why now: Reuters reported that Meta's Muse agent reached Signal messages on Mac and Android by using broad system permissions. Signal Foundation president Meredith Whittaker calls AI built into phone and computer operating systems an 'existential threat to Signal'"
  - "Background: Signal added a similar block on Windows 11 in May after Microsoft's Recall feature"
whyItMatters: "The new AI assistants on phones and computers work by 'looking' at your screen and acting for you. That's useful, but it also means a private conversation can end up being read and sent to an AI company's servers without you realizing. Signal is one of the first big apps to push back by default, and it's a reminder that your private messages are only as private as the assistant sitting on the same device."
consultingAngle: "If your team discusses clients, pricing, HR issues or anything confidential on their phones, write down which apps the AI assistants on company devices are allowed to see. In practice: keep sensitive conversations in apps with this kind of protection turned on, review which apps Siri AI, Gemini or Muse have permission to access, and turn off assistant access on devices that handle client data. It's a five-minute settings check per phone, and it's much easier to do now than to explain a leak later."
