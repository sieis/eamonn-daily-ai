keyDetails:
  - "What it is: an accounting plugin for ChatGPT plus connections to the software finance teams already use, released November 18"
  - "Connections named at launch include Intuit QuickBooks, Xero, Oracle NetSuite, BILL, FreshBooks, Rillet and Campfire"
  - "What it does: reads invoices and receipts, proposes journal entries and account categories, flags unclear details, gathers statements and payment records for reconciliation, and checks working papers for duplicates, missing information and unexplained differences"
  - "You can save templates and review steps so the same month-end process runs the same way next month"
  - "Humans stay in charge: entries are proposed, not posted, and OpenAI's guidance says to check the supporting evidence before approving anything"
  - "Several connections are listed as read-only, including Xero, BILL and FreshBooks. Xero says reviews happen in ChatGPT but any changes are made back in Xero, which keeps the record of changes. Xero's version is U.S.-only for now"
  - "Available on ChatGPT business plans (Business and Work, with Enterprise needing admin approval). Business and Enterprise data is excluded from model training by default. No separate price was announced"
whyItMatters: "Bookkeeping is one of the most common jobs small businesses want AI to help with, and until now that meant copying numbers out of your accounting software and pasting them into a chatbot. This connects ChatGPT directly to the tools that hold your books. It is designed to do the tedious first pass, like matching transactions and spotting gaps, while a person makes the final call."
consultingAngle: "If you or your bookkeeper already use QuickBooks or Xero and pay for ChatGPT Business, this is worth a test at your next month-end. Start with something low-risk: ask it to list unreconciled transactions, flag possible duplicate bills, or explain what changed in your profit and loss since last month. Save that as a template so it runs the same way every month. Keep connections read-only where possible and keep approval of every entry with a human, since errors in the books flow straight into your taxes. If you work with an outside accountant, ask whether they plan to use it, and agree on who reviews what. Accounting firms in particular should look at this to handle the client review work that piles up at month-end."
