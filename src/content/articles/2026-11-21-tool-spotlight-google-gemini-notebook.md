date: 2026-11-21
summary: "Gemini Notebook, the Google research tool known until July as NotebookLM, answers questions using only the documents you give it, with citations back to the source. As of November 16 it can also turn a notebook into an interactive site, like a dashboard or project tracker, that you can share. It's free to start and included in Google Workspace plans."
source: 'https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/11/transform-your-notes-and-research-into-dynamic-tools-with-Gemini-Notebook.html'

keyDetails:
  - "What it is: an AI notebook from Google. You upload your own sources, like PDFs, Google Docs, slides, website links and videos, and it answers questions based on those sources, pointing to where each answer came from"
  - "Who it's for: owners and teams who have a pile of documents (handbooks, SOPs, contracts, product specs, training materials) and keep answering the same questions about them"
  - "What it does: summarizes long documents, answers questions with citations, and creates study guides, FAQs, reports, slide decks and podcast-style Audio Overviews from your material"
  - "New this week: type 'make me a site' in the notebook chat to build an interactive site from your sources, like a sales dashboard, project tracker or visual guide, then open it in a new tab or share it. Rolling out on the web from November 16 (up to 15 days to appear), mobile coming later"
  - "Cost: free plan with limits (reported at 100 notebooks, 50 sources per notebook, 50 chat questions a day). Included in Google Workspace plans, and qualifying Workspace and Google AI plans get higher limits"
  - "For Workspace businesses, admins can turn Gemini Notebook on or off for users. There's no separate admin switch for the new sites feature"
  - "Watch out: one hands-on review reports that sharing a site also shares the underlying notebook and its sources, so check what's inside before you share"
whyItMatters: "Most small businesses have their know-how scattered across documents nobody reads. A tool that answers questions only from your own files, and shows you exactly where it found each answer, is far less likely to make things up than a general chatbot. The new sites feature lets you turn that same material into something a customer or new hire can click through."
consultingAngle: "Example: a 12-person cleaning or HVAC company onboards a new hire every month, and the office manager spends hours answering 'what's the procedure for...' questions. They put the employee handbook, safety procedures, pricing sheet and service checklists into one notebook. New hires ask it questions and get answers with a citation to the right page, and the manager asks it to 'make me a site' with a clickable first-week training guide. That can easily save the manager 3 to 5 hours per new hire. Keep customer personal data and anything confidential out of notebooks you plan to share, and review AI answers on safety or legal topics before relying on them."
