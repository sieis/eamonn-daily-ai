keyDetails:
  - "FBI flash advisory (reported November 16): attackers are using Artex, an AI-powered hacking tool first released as open source in July, against financial firms, government, tech, telecom, defense and law firms. Some attacks went from first contact to a break-in in under three hours"
  - "How it gets in: it scans for simple weaknesses such as missing multi-factor login and exposed or stolen passwords, then spreads to email, databases and other sensitive data. Weak defenses and no multi-factor login were blamed for breaches at South Korean financial firms that exposed at least 68,000 people's data"
  - "Barracuda, a security company, was breached through an old login belonging to a vendor it had stopped working with but never shut off"
  - "Google's Threat Intelligence Group (November 16) found a system it calls exploit_agent that watches for security updates to popular web software, works out the flaw being fixed, writes an attack and runs it, with no human involved"
  - "Google also describes attackers using AI-written exploits within days of a fix being announced, and one case where a foothold reached full administrator access within minutes"
  - "Anthropic is cutting its bug-disclosure window for high and critical issues from 90 days to 30 for bugs reported from January 1, 2027, because AI makes it more likely that someone else finds the same flaw. Actively exploited bugs keep a 7-day window"
whyItMatters: "For years, the advice was that hackers mostly chase big companies and that most small firms have weeks to install a security update. Both assumptions are breaking down. AI lets one attacker probe thousands of businesses at once, and it can turn a published fix into a working attack in a day or less. The good news is that these tools mostly exploit basic gaps, like missing two-step login and forgotten passwords, and those are cheap to close."
consultingAngle: "Four things to do this week, in order. First, turn on multi-factor login for email, banking, payroll and anything with customer data, for every person, with no exceptions. Second, set computers, phones, routers and your website software (including WordPress plugins) to install updates automatically, and if you cannot, decide who checks for updates every week. Third, list every outside vendor, contractor or former employee who still has a login to your systems and shut off the ones you no longer use. That is exactly how Barracuda was breached. Fourth, ask your IT provider or managed service provider how quickly they apply critical patches. If the answer is 'monthly,' that is now too slow. If you have no IT support at all, this is a good moment to get a one-time security review."
