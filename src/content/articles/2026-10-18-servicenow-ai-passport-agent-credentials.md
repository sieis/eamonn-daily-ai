keyDetails:
  - "ServiceNow announced AI Passport on October 15, with availability expected in Q4 2026"
  - "Each AI agent gets its own digitally signed credential that defines its identity, what it is allowed to do, which systems it can reach and which person is accountable for it"
  - "The agent checks the credential while it runs. If it steps outside its limits, the system can redirect it, restrict it or shut it off, and an organization can pause an agent by revoking its access"
  - "In ServiceNow's demo, a customer service agent was set up to approve refunds under $25. An $18 refund went through automatically, while a $40 request was blocked and sent to a person"
  - "It is built on open specifications so other identity and governance tools can issue and check credentials, including for outside vendors' agents. Nvidia is a design and launch partner"
  - "Caveat: this is based on the announcement and press coverage. Details may change before release, and analysts note that spending limits are a separate question from permissions"
whyItMatters: "AI agents are starting to act on their own: issuing refunds, sending emails, updating records. The obvious worry is what happens when one does something it shouldn't. A passport is a simple idea. Before an agent can act, it carries proof of what it is allowed to do, and the system refuses anything outside that. It is the same logic as giving a new employee a company card with a spending limit instead of unlimited access."
consultingAngle: "You don't need ServiceNow to borrow the idea. Before you let any AI tool take actions for you, write down three things: what it may do on its own (for example, refunds up to $25), what needs a person's approval (anything larger), and who is responsible for it. Then check whether your tools let you enforce those limits rather than just hoping. If you buy AI agents from outside vendors, ask what permission controls they offer and whether you can see a record of what the agent did. Larger companies running many agents should treat 'who is this agent and what can it touch?' as a procurement question now, not after the first incident."
