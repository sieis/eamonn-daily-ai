whyItMatters: "AI isn't only a tool for bosses. Your employees have the same chatbots you do, and they're using them to write sharper, more detailed complaints and push back on decisions that affect them. That's not bad in itself, since it helps people who can't afford a lawyer explain their case. But it means a hastily written policy, a vague termination letter or a cut you can't justify will get picked apart faster than before. The survey is European, but the same tools are available to workers everywhere."
consultingAngle: "Assume any HR decision you make could be reviewed by an employee with an AI assistant that knows employment law. Practically: document the reasons behind layoffs, schedule changes and performance decisions clearly and consistently. Have someone check that your handbook matches what you actually do, since AI is good at spotting the gaps. If you're cutting or slowing hiring because of AI, be honest about it, and plan how you'll retrain the people who stay. Finally, name one person who owns AI rules in your business, even if you only have 15 employees. Only a quarter of the larger companies in this survey have done that. If you employ people in the EU, check what the AI Act requires before you use AI in hiring or reviews."
