date: 2026-11-25
summary: "A survey of 9,750 business leaders across 10 Asia-Pacific markets, run by Strand Partners for Amazon Web Services, found 58% of companies using AI say it has already earned back its initial cost. Yet only 12% are very confident they can measure that return accurately, and fewer than 1 in 5 have a formal AI strategy. Companies with a strategy were more than four times as likely to reach the most advanced stage of adoption."

keyDetails:
  - "Who and how many: 9,750 business leaders in Australia, Indonesia, Japan, Malaysia, New Zealand, the Philippines, Singapore, South Korea, Thailand and Vietnam, surveyed by Strand Partners for AWS"
  - "Payback: 58% of AI-using businesses say their AI spending has already paid back its upfront cost"
  - "Measurement gap: only 12% are very confident they can accurately measure their return on AI"
  - "Strategy pays: just 19% have a formal, comprehensive AI strategy, but 35% of those reached the most advanced adoption stage, versus 8% of businesses without one"
  - "Off-the-shelf tools dominate: 64% still rely mainly on general-purpose or ready-made AI tools for specific tasks"
  - "Biggest barrier: 48% cite a shortage of AI and digital skills"
  - "Adoption keeps rising: every market grew year over year, led by Australia (61%), Singapore (59%) and South Korea (58%)"
  - "Keep in mind: the survey was commissioned by AWS, which sells AI services, and the payback figures are self-reported"
whyItMatters: "This lines up with what U.S. surveys have been showing all fall: most businesses think AI is paying off, but very few can prove it with numbers. 'It feels like we save time' is a fine start, but it won't hold up when you're deciding whether to renew a subscription or expand to the whole team. The companies pulling ahead are the ones that wrote down a plan and decided what they were measuring."
consultingAngle: "You don't need a 30-page AI strategy, but you do need a one-pager: the three tasks you're using AI for, how long each took before, how long it takes now, and what you're paying for the tools. Track it monthly in a simple spreadsheet. That turns 'we think it's working' into 'it saves us 12 hours a week for $60 a month,' which is the number that justifies the next investment, or tells you to cancel. And since skills are the top barrier, budget an afternoon of training for every tool you roll out; ready-made tools only pay off if people actually know how to use them."
