  - "What happened: investigators at South Korea's Fair Trade Commission (KFTC) referred Apple and Apple Korea for review over how the iPhone 16's AI features were advertised"
  - "The features in question: Genmoji, Image Playground, and Siri's ability to understand your personal context and what's on your screen"
  - "The gap: according to the investigators' report, Siri's personal-context features were advertised for Korea in early April 2025 but didn't arrive until October 2026, about 18 months later. The probe centers on whether Apple clearly explained what was available and when"
  - "Apple's response: Apple says it cooperated with the investigation, disagrees with the conclusions in the examiner's report, points to Korean-language support it added, and plans to present its defense to the Commission"
  - "What's next: the Commission hasn't issued a ruling or a penalty yet"
  - "The pattern: in the U.S., an ad watchdog pushed Apple to drop an 'available now' claim in April 2025, and Apple agreed in May 2026 to a $250 million class-action settlement over the same iPhone 16 AI marketing. That settlement's claim deadline is December 21"
whyItMatters: "Many people bought an iPhone 16 partly because of AI features shown in ads, only to wait a year or more for them. Regulators in several countries are now treating 'coming soon' AI marketing as a consumer-protection issue. If you're shopping for AI-powered gadgets or software this holiday season, buy based on what works today, not what's promised for later."
consultingAngle: "This is a warning for any business that markets AI features. If you advertise an AI capability, whether in a product, a service package or a sales pitch, make sure it actually works now, or label clearly that it's coming later and when. Regulators are looking at the overall impression an ad gives, and fine print may not save you. The same goes for buying: when a software vendor pitches AI features, ask which ones are live today and get the roadmap in writing before you sign a contract."
