date: 2026-11-23
summary: "Ollama is a free app for Mac, Windows and Linux that downloads open AI models and runs them right on your own machine, with a simple chat window, no subscription and no usage limits. After this week's report that some AI resellers were reading customers' prompts, it's the easiest way for a small business to keep sensitive work completely private. New models like Thinking Machines' Inkling 2 Mini, released November 21, now run well on a 32GB Mac."
source: 'https://ollama.com/pricing'
sourceName: 'Ollama'

keyDetails:
  - "What it is: a free, open-source desktop app (macOS, Windows, Linux) that lets you pick an open AI model, like Qwen, Gemma, DeepSeek or Inkling, download it, and chat with it offline"
  - "Who it's for: owners and teams who handle confidential information, like client files, contracts, health or financial records, and want AI help without sending that data to anyone"
  - "What it does: summarizes and drafts documents, answers questions about files you drop in, helps write and review code, and works with other apps through a local connection"
  - "Cost: running models on your own computer is free and, in Ollama's words, 'always unlimited.' Optional cloud plans for bigger models run $20/month (Pro), $100/month (Max) and $500/month (Team, early access). Ollama says cloud prompts are never logged or trained on"
  - "New option this week: Inkling 2 Mini from Thinking Machines Lab (Apache 2.0 license, free for business use) needs about 20GB of memory and runs through Ollama on Macs with at least 32GB. Independent testers at Artificial Analysis say it matches a model with five times more active computing power while being faster and cheaper"
  - "The tradeoff: local models are smaller than ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini. They're very good at summarizing, drafting and sorting, but weaker at hard reasoning and long, complex instructions"
  - "Hardware matters: a recent Mac with 16GB of memory can run small models. 32GB or more gives you much better ones"
whyItMatters: "Every time you paste something into a regular AI chatbot, it travels to someone else's servers. For most tasks that's fine. For a client's legal file or an employee's medical note, it may not be. Ollama makes 'private AI' something a non-technical person can set up in about ten minutes, with nothing leaving the computer."
consultingAngle: "Example: a five-person accounting or bookkeeping firm wants AI help but promised clients their financial records never go to outside services. They install Ollama on the office's 32GB Mac Studio and download a model. Staff drop in a client's bank statements and messy notes and ask for a summary of unusual transactions and a draft of the follow-up email. That turns a 45-minute review into about 15 minutes, saving several hours a week across the team, and the firm can honestly tell clients nothing was uploaded. Use a cloud AI tool on a business plan for the harder, non-sensitive work, and keep Ollama for the files that should never leave the building."
