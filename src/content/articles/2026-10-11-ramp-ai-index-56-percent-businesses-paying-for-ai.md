  - "Ramp's index tracks real purchases of AI tools through its payment platform, covering more than 70,000 businesses. Its August reading was 56.1%, up 0.4 points from July. Coverage described the August gain as a slowdown"
  - "The Census Bureau's survey asks whether a business used AI in any business function and has recently put adoption at roughly 20% to 22%. The two numbers differ because Ramp counts paid subscriptions among its own customers, who skew toward tech-comfortable companies, while Census surveys all kinds of firms"
  - "Ramp reports a median AI spend of about $12.50 per employee per month across firms. A separate Ramp and Revelio study found that firms investing most heavily in AI grew headcount by roughly 10% after adoption, while light adopters saw no significant change"
  - "Ramp's next AI Index release is scheduled for October 14, 2026"
whyItMatters: "You will see headlines claiming everyone from 20% to 75% of businesses use AI, and they are all 'right' because they measure different things. The honest takeaway is that paying for AI is now normal for a majority of companies that use modern payment tools, while a much smaller share of all businesses have made it part of daily work. If you feel behind, you are probably not as far behind as the loudest numbers suggest. If you feel ahead, there is still room for competitors to catch up."
consultingAngle: "Use these numbers to sanity-check your own position, not to panic. A simple benchmark: roughly $12.50 per employee per month is what a typical business spends on AI tools. Add up what you spend now, then ask whether each subscription is tied to a real task such as drafting, scheduling, bookkeeping or support. Cut tools nobody uses and double down on the one or two that save measurable hours. Watch the October 14 update to see whether adoption is plateauing or just pausing."
