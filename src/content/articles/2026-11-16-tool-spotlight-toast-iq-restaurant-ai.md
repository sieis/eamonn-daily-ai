keyDetails:
  - "What it is: an AI assistant built into Toast, a publicly traded company whose registers, kitchen screens and online ordering are used by restaurants, cafes and bars across the U.S."
  - "Who it's for: owners and managers already on Toast who want answers from their sales and labor data without building reports"
  - "What it does: ask questions such as 'which items sold worst on weekday lunches last month' and get an answer drawn from your own sales, labor, guest and menu data. Toast says you can also change a price, mark an item out of stock or update the menu from the chat"
  - "Toast IQ Team (paid add-on): a Scheduling Agent drafts the weekly schedule from staff availability, expected sales and overtime risk. Toast says restaurants in its beta averaged 41% fewer overtime hours per employee, which is the company's own figure"
  - "Toast IQ Grow (paid add-on): marketing agents that suggest and run email campaigns and offers with your approval, ask guests for reviews and monitor how the restaurant shows up in search"
  - "Pricing reported by Nation's Restaurant News in October: Team from $60 a month plus $9 per employee, Grow from $699 a month. Toast says access to the core assistant depends on your plan and setup, so confirm with your Toast rep"
  - "Good to know: it only works inside Toast, so it is not a reason on its own to switch systems. A phone answering agent and a menu agent were in early access as of October"
whyItMatters: "Restaurants run on thin margins and very little spare time. Most owners have years of sales data sitting in the register and no hours to dig through it. An assistant that answers a question in a sentence makes that data usable on a busy Tuesday."
consultingAngle: "Example: a cafe owner with 12 staff spends about two hours every Sunday building the schedule and still gets caught by overtime. With the Scheduling Agent, the first draft is ready in seconds, based on who is available and how busy each shift is expected to be. The owner spends 15 minutes adjusting it and publishes. At $60 plus $9 per employee, that is about $168 a month, so it pays for itself if it saves a few overtime shifts. Start with the questions, which carry no risk: ask what your slowest hours and weakest menu items are, and act on one finding. Before letting it change prices or send marketing on its own, keep approvals switched on and check its first few schedules against what you would have written. It does not know that two of your staff should not close together."
