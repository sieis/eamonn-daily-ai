whyItMatters: "Texting is something everyone already knows how to do, which makes these assistants far easier to pick up than most AI apps. The tradeoff is access: to be useful, they need your email, calendar and sometimes your payment details, and many come from young startups. Before you hand one the keys, check who makes it, what it can do without asking you, and how to cut off its access."
consultingAngle: "Assume some of your employees are already using one of these, because they are cheap and easy. That's fine for personal errands, but a texting agent connected to a work email or calendar can see client details. Add a line to your AI policy: personal AI assistants should not be connected to company email, calendars or files unless the business approves the tool. For owners who live on their phones, such as contractors, salon owners or field service teams, a texting assistant can be a real time saver for scheduling and follow-ups. Pick an option with a business plan, an 'ask before acting' setting, and clear terms on data, and start by letting it draft rather than send."
