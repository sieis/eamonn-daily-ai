keyDetails:
  - "Survey of 1,731 owners of U.S. businesses with 24 or fewer employees, published by payroll company Gusto"
  - "67% use AI tools: 19% are experimenting, 28% use it for some tasks, 15% use it regularly across several parts of the business and 5% have it built in wherever feasible"
  - "Professional services lead on deeper use at 27%, compared with 16% in personal services and 13% in community services"
  - "25% of businesses with employees have an AI policy, up from 15% last year"
  - "Owners are most comfortable letting AI handle communication tasks with light human review, and much less comfortable with AI analyzing data or answering customers directly. About 60% of AI users are comfortable with AI drafting marketing content, compared with 27% of non-users"
  - "Wider context: 61% are optimistic about their own business, but only 18% are optimistic about the U.S. economy, down from 29%"
whyItMatters: "This is a picture of the smallest businesses, the ones with a few employees and no IT department. It shows AI has become normal there, but mostly as a helper for writing and messages. It also shows a gap: three in four employers have given staff no guidance on what they can and cannot do with AI."
consultingAngle: "If you use AI for a few tasks and nothing more, you are in the majority, not behind. Two practical steps stand out from this data. First, write a one-page AI policy. It only needs to cover which tools are approved, what must never be pasted in (customer data, payroll details, passwords) and what a person must check before it goes out. Second, move from scattered use to one or two repeatable jobs, such as drafting customer follow-ups or summarizing weekly sales, and measure the hours saved. Keep in mind that the survey comes from a payroll software company, so the owners who answered may be more comfortable with technology than average."
