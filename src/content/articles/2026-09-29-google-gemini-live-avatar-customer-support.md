keyDetails:
  - "Announced September 24, the feature pairs live speech with generated video, so customers see an avatar talk, react and change facial expressions during the conversation"
  - "It supports 97 languages and can switch between them mid-conversation, with lip-sync that adapts to the new language"
  - "The agent can call other software in the background, such as searching inventory or looking up an order, while it keeps talking. It can also see a customer's live camera or shared screen"
  - "Early users include Cox Automotive (Autotrader) for a shopping assistant, Equal AI (over 1 million calls a day across nine Indian languages), and Salesforce, which is integrating it with Agentforce"
  - "Custom avatars require enterprise approval, and all generated audio and video carries an invisible SynthID watermark so it can be identified as AI-made"
whyItMatters: "For most people, customer service AI has meant a chat box or a robotic phone menu. This is a step toward AI that looks and sounds like a person on a video call. That could make support faster and available around the clock, but it also raises fair questions about honesty: customers should know when they're talking to an AI, which is part of why Google adds watermarks."
consultingAngle: "This is enterprise-grade for now, but it signals where customer-facing AI is heading. If you run a business with a lot of repetitive support or sales questions, especially across multiple languages, it's worth watching. Before adopting anything like it, decide what the AI is allowed to do on its own, when it hands off to a human, and how you'll tell customers it's an AI."
