title: "Gallup: AI Is Cutting Research and Writing Time, but Not Meetings or Customer Work"
date: 2026-11-17
summary: "A new Gallup study of U.S. workers finds 32% now use AI a few times a week or more, and most of them say it has changed how they spend their day. The time saved comes mainly from gathering information and drafting, while meetings, teamwork and customer contact are almost untouched."
source: 'https://gallup.com/workplace/716585/ai-changing-employees-spend-time-work.aspx'
sourceName: 'Gallup'

keyDetails:
  - "Survey of U.S. employees fielded August 5 to 19, 2026, published November 16. Gallup lists a sample of 21,648 for the main study"
  - "53% of employees use AI at least a few times a year. 32% are frequent users (a few times a week or more), and 16% of all employees use it daily"
  - "Remote-capable workers are far more likely to be frequent users (49%) than on-site workers (22%)"
  - "Among frequent users, 88% say AI has changed how they spend their time. They spend less time gathering information (52%), writing or drafting (47%), analyzing data (38%) and on admin tasks (29%)"
  - "Reviewing and correcting work went both ways: 21% spend more time on it, 23% less"
  - "Little has changed for meetings (83% say no change), working with customers (82%) and collaborating with coworkers (78%)"
  - "Only 29% of employees at companies using AI say it has raised expectations for daily output. But 33% of frequent users feel pressure to use AI to keep up, versus 17% of infrequent users"
whyItMatters: "This is one of the clearest pictures yet of what AI actually does at work. It is not replacing whole jobs for most people. It is taking the slow first draft and the digging-around-for-information parts off people's plates. The human parts of work, such as talking to customers and working with colleagues, look the same as before. And the gap between desk workers and on-site workers is big, so the benefits are not spreading evenly."
consultingAngle: "Use this as a map for where to start. If you are wondering where AI will pay off first in your business, look at the research, drafting, data and admin work your team does, because that is where real users report savings. Then ask the follow-up question most businesses skip: what happens to the time saved? If no one decides, it tends to disappear into more email. Pick one or two higher-value uses, such as more customer follow-ups or faster quotes. Watch the 'reviewing and correcting' line too, because someone still has to check AI output, and that time is real. Finally, if you have on-site or frontline staff, do not assume they are covered. Gallup's numbers suggest they are being left behind, so they may need tools built for their work, like scheduling or job notes on a phone."
