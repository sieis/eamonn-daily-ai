keyDetails:
  - "The study: Adecco Group's seventh Global Workforce of the Future report, released November 19, surveyed 40,500 workers in 36 countries, plus Adecco placement data from 23 markets"
  - "About three in four workers now use AI at work (74%, up from 64% in last year's survey, which had a smaller sample)"
  - "Where AI is built into everyday workflows rather than bolted on, workers report saving 20% more time per day and are more likely to spend it on higher-value work"
  - "96% of workers who are involved in redesigning their roles say they are willing to adapt, compared with 29% of those left out"
  - "Only 39% of organizations involve workers in job redesign, 36% explain how AI creates opportunities for them, and 19% of workers say their leaders are empathetic to the effects of change"
  - "Workers who completed AI training are 51 percentage points more likely to feel comfortable working alongside AI, yet formal training completion fell 10 points in a year"
  - "Adecco's placement data shows entry-level placements in AI-exposed roles fell 2.2% year over year, while demand for highly skilled white-collar profiles rose 28%"
whyItMatters: "Most people now use AI at work in some form. This study shows that the tool alone doesn't decide whether it helps. What matters is whether the people doing the work get a say in how it changes their day, and whether they get trained. When they do, nearly everyone is willing to adapt. When they don't, most push back. It's a useful reminder for anyone worried about AI and their job: the workplaces handling this well are the ones talking openly with staff about it."
consultingAngle: "This is the cheapest AI advantage available to a small business, because you are close to your team already. Before rolling out a new AI tool, sit down with the people who will use it and ask two questions: which parts of your week are repetitive, and which parts need your judgment? Let them help choose where AI goes first, set aside an hour of real training rather than sending a link, and agree up front what the saved time will be used for (more customer calls, faster quotes, leaving on time). Then check in after 30 days. The numbers here suggest that the conversation itself is what turns a tool purchase into time actually saved."
