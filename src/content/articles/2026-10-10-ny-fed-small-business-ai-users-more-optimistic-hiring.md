date: 2026-10-10
summary: "A new New York Fed analysis of its Small Business Credit Survey finds that firms already using AI are more likely to expect higher employment and revenue over the next year than non-users, even after accounting for firm size, owner traits and location. It shows an association, not proof that AI causes growth."
source: 'https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/10/ai-adoption-and-employment-expectations-evidence-from-a-survey-of-small-business-owners/'

keyDetails:
  - "Published October 8, 2026 on the New York Fed's Liberty Street Economics blog, using the 2025 Small Business Credit Survey (fielded September to November 2025, so the data is about a year old)"
  - "46% of firms with at least one employee said they or their staff were using AI tools, and another 15% planned to start within 12 months"
  - "Among firms using AI, 63% called it at least somewhat important to producing their goods or services, and 51% said they had integrated it partly or fully into business processes"
  - "Firms already using AI were more likely than non-users to forecast higher employment and higher revenue over the next year, even after controlling for firm, owner and location characteristics"
  - "Caveat: this is optimism about the future, not measured results. Growth-minded owners may simply be more likely to try new tools, so the survey cannot show that AI caused the difference"
  - "Other 2026 surveys report different adoption rates (for example, the U.S. Chamber's 66%) because they define 'using AI' and sample businesses differently"
whyItMatters: "Public debate about AI and jobs is mostly fear versus hype. This is a neutral, central-bank-affiliated look at what small business owners themselves expect, and it points toward growth and hiring rather than cuts. The honest limit: it measures expectations, and it cannot say whether AI is the reason. Still, it is a useful counterweight to headlines that assume every business using AI is shrinking its team."
consultingAngle: "If you are weighing AI for your business, the useful takeaway is that roughly half of small employers are already past 'thinking about it,' and about half of those have built AI into real processes, not just tried a chatbot. Pick one repeatable process (quotes, scheduling, customer replies), measure the hours it takes today, and track the change after rolling out a tool. That gives you your own evidence instead of relying on surveys, which differ widely on how many companies 'use AI.'"
