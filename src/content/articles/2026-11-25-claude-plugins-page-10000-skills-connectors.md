It's live now on Free, Pro, Max and Ultra plans, with Team and Enterprise 'coming soon'. Public plugins are labeled Verified or Community, a distinction worth paying attention to."

keyDetails:
  - "What changed: the separate Skills, Connectors and Plugins tabs are now one Plugins page. Anthropic's announcement: 'There's now one way to connect your tools to Claude, teach it a new skill, or do both'"
  - "Two kinds of add-ons: connectors link Claude to outside accounts such as email, calendars or company databases; skills are instruction packs, like one that builds a spreadsheet in a set format. A plugin can bundle both"
  - "How to use it: filter the page by type, then use a skill by clicking '+' or typing '/' in a chat. Anything you already installed stays installed"
  - "Trust labels: public plugins are marked either Verified or Community. Plugins from your company or shared by colleagues show up first under 'Your plugins'"
  - "Availability: Free, Pro, Max and Ultra plans now; Team and Enterprise listed as coming soon with no date"
  - "Watch out: removing a plugin does not disconnect a connector bundled inside it, so disconnect the connector separately. From November 30, organizations that have turned off Skills will also have plugins blocked in the Claude desktop app"
  - "Health data: connectors, plugins and skills aren't covered by Claude's health-privacy agreement, so keep patient information out of them"
  - "Also new this week: Claude artifacts, the mini-apps Claude builds in chat, can now be installed as desktop apps on Mac and Windows"
whyItMatters: "Plugins are how an AI assistant goes from a smart chat box to something that can actually work with your email, files and business tools. Putting everything in one place makes that much easier to find. But 10,000 options also means a lot of add-ons built by people other than Anthropic, and some of them get access to your accounts. Treat a plugin like any app you'd install on your phone: check who made it and what it can touch."
consultingAngle: "This is a good moment to set a simple plugin policy, even if you're a team of five: start with Verified plugins for the tools you already pay for (calendar, email, drive, CRM), write down who approved each connector, and review the list once a quarter. Skills are the low-risk win: turn the reports, proposals or checklists your team builds the same way every week into a skill so Claude formats them consistently. If you're on a Team or Enterprise plan, use the wait before it rolls out to decide which plugins you'll allow."
