Its president, Meredith Whittaker, says the fix is incomplete and is asking Apple, Google and Microsoft for a proper way for apps to opt out of AI access."

keyDetails:
  - "Announced November 20 and 21: Signal is adding the same screenshot block it already uses on Windows (to stop Microsoft Recall) to its iPhone and Android apps"
  - "iPhone: a new 'Block Siri AI' setting in iOS 27 is on by default. It stops Siri AI from scanning Signal, blocks screenshots and hides your chats in the app switcher. You can turn it off, or pause it for 10 minutes to take a screenshot"
  - "Android: Signal automatically turns on its Screen security setting when it detects an AI assistant trying to read the screen, on Android versions where it can detect that reliably"
  - "Mac: Signal says it can't block AI assistants there yet. Its advice is to add Signal to the list under System Settings > Siri > Excluded Apps"
  - "Whittaker called the fix 'incomplete' because blocking screenshots doesn't stop an AI agent from reaching app data in other ways, and said turning the protection off 'would break our privacy guarantees' for everyone in the conversation"
  - "The move follows a November 18 Reuters investigation that found Meta's Muse agent pulled in the text of Signal messages on two of three test devices, using permissions granted during setup. Meta disputes that reading of the tests"
  - "Signal is asking Apple, Google and Microsoft for a simple, documented way for apps to mark themselves off-limits to AI agents"
whyItMatters: "The newest AI assistants work by looking at whatever is on your screen, so they can help you with any app. That is useful, but it also means a private conversation can end up inside an AI system, and you may not have agreed to that. Neither has the person on the other end. Signal is the first big app to say no by default. Expect banking, health and other sensitive apps to make similar choices, and expect some of your AI assistant's 'see my screen' features to stop working in certain apps."
consultingAngle: "If your team uses Siri AI, Gemini, Copilot, Claude or ChatGPT features that can read the screen or control the computer, ask a simple question: what is on our screens? Client files, payroll, patient details and private chats can all be captured. Three practical steps: make a short list of apps that AI assistants should never see (password manager, banking, HR, any client portal) and use the 'excluded apps' or block-list settings on Mac, iPhone and in tools like Claude's computer use. Tell staff that if a client conversation must stay private, it should happen in an app that blocks AI capture. And if you build or buy customer-facing apps, ask your vendor whether they support blocking AI screen capture, because your customers will start asking."
