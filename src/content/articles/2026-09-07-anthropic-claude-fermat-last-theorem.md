  - "Anthropic let Claude work largely autonomously for 11 days on an open collaborative platform to formalize Andrew Wiles' 1994 proof of Fermat's Last Theorem into Lean, a language computers can automatically check for errors"
  - "The result: roughly 13 million lines of Lean code and about 30,300 intermediate theorems (29,500 used in the final proof) — reportedly the largest file of its kind ever produced"
  - "Mathematicians had expected this formalization effort, underway since 2024 and led by Imperial College London's Kevin Buzzard, to take several more years; Claude finished it in under two weeks"
  - "The proof was checked against nothing but Lean's three foundational axioms, which rules out the kind of human transcription error that can creep into a traditional written proof"
  - "Buzzard, who has run his own years-long human-led formalization project on the same theorem, wrote on his blog that Anthropic 'beat him to it,' calling it 'an extraordinary autoformalization achievement' — while noting the proof itself contains no new mathematics, only a faithful translation of Wiles' known proof"
