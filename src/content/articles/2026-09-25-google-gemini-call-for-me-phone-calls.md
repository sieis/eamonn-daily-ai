keyDetails:
  - "'Call for Me' places an actual phone call from the user's own number, using the beta Phone by Google app, to book appointments, reschedule reservations, or check whether an item is in stock"
  - "A live transcript appears on screen as the call happens, and the person can jump in and take over the conversation at any point"
  - "It's rolling out first to paid Gemini subscribers on the Pixel 11, 11 Pro, 11 Pro XL, and 11 Pro Fold who are enrolled in the Phone by Google public beta, US only for now"
  - "Google has built in limits: it can't dial 911, complete a payment, share sensitive personal information, or make telemarketing-style calls"
  - "The move mirrors a similar calling feature already shipping in Meta's new Muse AI agent, putting two of the biggest consumer AI players into the same 'AI calls a business for you' race at once"
whyItMatters: "This is one of the clearest signs yet that AI is moving from something you type into a chat box to something that makes real phone calls and takes real actions in the world. For a general audience, the more immediate effect isn't using it yourself yet — it's that any business that takes bookings by phone should expect a growing share of incoming calls to be an AI, not the customer, on the other end of the line."
consultingAngle: "Any client who runs a phone-based business — a salon, restaurant, repair shop, clinic — should hear about this now, not after it becomes common: it's worth a quick check of how their phone tree, hold system, and front-desk staff would handle an AI caller asking direct, efficient questions rather than a chattier human customer. On the flip side, this is also a preview of a low-cost virtual receptionist capability those same owners could eventually turn around and use for their own outbound calls and confirmations."
