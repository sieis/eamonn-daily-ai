keyDetails:
  - "Apple's legal team filed evidence alleging former Apple engineer Chang Liu, who left to join OpenAI, used his old company-issued MacBook to download a highly confidential Apple circuit schematic before departing"
  - 'Apple says Liu then had an AI agent autonomously run simulations on that schematic in circuit-design software, and argues that once confidential data is fed into a system that "learns" from it, the exposure can become effectively impossible to undo'
  - 'Apple also alleges Liu texted an OpenAI colleague instructions for destroying evidence after learning he was under investigation, and that the colleague confirmed she would comply'
  - "OpenAI has pushed back, arguing at a recent hearing that Apple hasn't shown proof any confidential information was actually misused"
  - 'The case is part of a broader dispute: Apple has said in earlier filings that additional former employees may have taken confidential data to OpenAI, and the litigation is ongoing'
whyItMatters: "Big companies suing each other over stolen secrets isn't new, but the AI angle here is the notable part: Apple's argument is specifically that running confidential company data through an AI agent can spread that information in ways that are hard to trace or reverse. It's a real, concrete illustration of a risk that usually only gets discussed in the abstract."
consultingAngle: "This is a useful cautionary tale for any team using AI tools day to day, not just companies the size of Apple: before an employee pastes a client contract, a product design, or financial data into a general AI tool or agent to 'have it help,' check whether that tool retains or trains on your data, and put a simple written policy in place about what information is and isn't allowed to go into AI tools your business doesn't fully control."
