keyDetails:
  - "California's AI Transparency Act (SB 942) became operative on August 2, 2026, after its original January 1, 2026 start date was pushed back to line up with the EU AI Act's own transparency deadline"
  - "It applies to any publicly available generative AI system with more than 1 million monthly users in California — effectively every major chatbot, image generator, and video tool most businesses already use"
  - "Covered providers must invisibly mark AI-generated images, video, and audio, offer a free public tool to check whether content came from their system, and let users add a visible 'AI-generated' label if they choose"
  - "Providers must also contractually require any company that licenses their AI system to keep that invisible marking intact — so parts of the obligation flow down to businesses building on these tools, not just the AI companies themselves"
  - "Violations carry a $5,000 penalty per instance, with each day a violation continues counting separately, and further requirements phase in through 2028"
whyItMatters: "Most people who use AI to write a quick email or generate an image have no idea their content is being invisibly tagged behind the scenes, or that a state law now requires it. As more of these disclosure rules roll out state by state, it's becoming genuinely useful to understand what the AI tools people use every day are quietly doing with their content."
