summary: "Anthropic's new 'Install as app' beta lets anyone with a Claude account take an artifact, the small tool or dashboard Claude builds in a chat, and install it as its own desktop app with an icon, a window, notifications and menu bar access. Apps can use your Claude connectors and update automatically when you edit them, with no coding, packaging or API keys required."

keyDetails:
  - "What it is: a one-click way to install a Claude artifact (a mini-app Claude writes for you in chat, like a calculator, tracker or dashboard) as a standalone desktop app with its own icon and window, outside the Claude chat"
  - "How to do it: update the Claude app for Mac or Windows, open an artifact, then choose 'Install as app' from the Share menu. There's nothing to package or sign, and no API key or separate login"
  - "What the apps can do: use Claude and your connected services (such as your calendar or Google Drive), send notifications, sit in the Mac menu bar or Windows system tray, and update automatically whenever the artifact is edited"
  - "Three permission levels: by default an app can only do what a web page could. You can grant extra features like notifications and keyboard shortcuts, or full computer access that lets it run programs and change files. Full access is only allowed for apps you, or your company on Team and Enterprise, made"
  - "Availability: beta on all Claude plans, including free. It's on by default for Team plans (with full access off) and must be switched on by an owner on Enterprise. Not available for HIPAA-ready organizations"
  - "Costs: any request an installed app makes to Claude counts toward your plan's usage limits"
  - "Limits: you must be online and signed in to open an app, Claude Docs, Slides and Sheets can't be installed, and Anthropic doesn't review apps before you install them"
whyItMatters: "Until now, the handy little tools Claude could build for you lived inside a chat window and were easy to lose. Now you can describe a tool in plain English, like 'a tracker for my daily deliveries' or 'a timer that reminds me to follow up with leads', and end up with something that sits on your computer like any other app. That puts custom software within reach of people who have never written a line of code."
consultingAngle: "Start with one small, annoying task that currently lives in a spreadsheet or on sticky notes: a job-quote calculator, a daily cash-drawer checklist, or a dashboard that pulls today's appointments from your calendar. Have Claude build it, use it in the browser for a week, then install it. Keep apps at the default permission level unless there's a clear reason not to, only grant full computer access to tools your own team built, and remember that heavy use draws down your Claude plan's limits. If you're on a Team or Enterprise plan, decide now who is allowed to build and share these apps so useful tools spread and risky ones don't."
