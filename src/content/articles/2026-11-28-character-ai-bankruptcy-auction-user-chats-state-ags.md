keyDetails:
  - "What's for sale: user data, including chat histories from more than 21 million U.S. accounts (over 2.1 million belonging to minors), plus other personal data such as IP addresses"
  - "Why: Character.AI, the AI companion chatbot app, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in October 2026 after its co-founders returned to Google and it faced child-safety lawsuits"
  - "The only disclosed bidder: Smirks and Pangs Acquisition LLC, an anonymous Delaware company formed November 13, 2026. Its plans for the chats are unclear"
  - "Who's objecting: 42 state attorneys general, including California, Connecticut, Maryland, Minnesota and New York. California AG Rob Bonta said, 'Character.AI promised consumers that their data would not be sold'"
  - "Their concerns: a buyer wouldn't have to honor Character.AI's privacy policy and could resell the data without telling anyone. New chatbot safety laws in California, Colorado, New York and Connecticut take effect January 1, 2027"
  - "Character.AI's position: its blog says users' information will still be protected under its privacy policy. A separate $20 million deal giving Disney access to its intellectual property, but not user chats, is still awaiting court approval"
whyItMatters: "People tell AI chatbots things they'd never post publicly: health worries, relationship problems, money trouble, work secrets. This case shows that a company's privacy promise can be challenged once it goes bankrupt, because in bankruptcy customer data is treated as an asset that can be sold to pay creditors. Whatever the court decides, it's a reminder that what you type into an AI app may outlive the app itself."
consultingAngle: "Add 'what happens to our data if you go out of business?' to your AI vendor checklist. Many AI tools your team uses are run by young, venture-funded startups, and not all of them will survive. Before you put customer records, contracts or internal plans into any AI tool, check three things: whether you can export and delete your data on demand, whether the contract stops them from selling it (including in a merger or bankruptcy), and whether there's a business plan with stronger terms than the free one. For sensitive work, stick with established providers on business agreements, and clear out old chats you no longer need."
