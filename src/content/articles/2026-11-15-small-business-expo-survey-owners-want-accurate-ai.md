title: "Small Business Owners Want AI That Is Accurate, Easy and Works With Their Tools, Not Cheaper AI"
date: 2026-11-15
summary: "A Small Business Expo survey of 764 respondents found that 71% want AI tools to be more accurate, easier to use or better connected to the software they already run. Lower cost came last. Owners who have not adopted AI yet mostly said they need training or lower prices first."
source: 'https://www.thesmallbusinessexpo.com/blog/ai-for-small-businesses/'
sourceName: 'Small Business Expo'
company: 'Industry/Other'
audience: 'SMB'
formatFit: 'Short-form'
priority: 'Medium'
keyDetails:
  - "Published November 13 by the research team at the Small Business Expo, a trade show organizer, based on 764 respondents"
  - "Top requested improvements: better accuracy (25.0%), ease of use (23.3%), integration with existing tools (22.9%), more training and resources (15.8%) and lower costs (13.0%)"
  - "Content creation is the most common main use of AI, cited by 28.4%"
  - "Owners using AI to analyze data care most about accuracy: 51.4% of them picked it, against 19.2% of everyone else. Owners using AI to automate tasks were most likely to want better integration (35.0%)"
  - "The split between users and non-users is stark: 69.2% of non-users chose training or lower costs, compared with 24.2% of current users"
  - "Caveat: the survey's methodology is thinly documented, so treat the figures as a useful signal rather than a precise measure"
whyItMatters: "This lines up with what bigger studies have been saying all autumn. Most small businesses have tried AI, but few rely on it for serious work, and the reason is trust. People will not hand over the numbers or the customer emails to a tool that sometimes makes things up or that lives in a separate window from the rest of their software."
consultingAngle: "Two takeaways for owners. If you have not started, the barrier most people name is knowing how, not money, so a few hours of hands-on training on one real task will do more than shopping for a cheaper tool. If you already use AI, the next step is usually connecting it to the systems you already pay for, such as your accounting software, CRM or email, rather than adding another standalone app. And wherever accuracy matters, like pricing, payroll or anything a customer will read, build in a check: a person reviews the output, or the AI has to show where each number came from."
