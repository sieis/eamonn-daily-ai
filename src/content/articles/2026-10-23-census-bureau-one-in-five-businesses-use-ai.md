keyDetails:
  - "The Census Bureau's Business Trends and Outlook Survey found overall AI use hovering between 17% and 20% from December 2025 to May 2026, with 20% to 23% expecting to use it within six months"
  - "A WeWork analysis of the same data reports 21.7% of businesses using AI as of July 2026, up from 17.7% at the start of the year. Treat that figure as secondhand"
  - "Use rises with size: 37% of firms with 250+ employees report using AI, compared with under 20% of firms with four or fewer employees"
  - "Office-based sectors lead: Information at 43.7%, Professional Services 37.6%, Finance and Insurance 36.0%"
  - "By contrast, Intuit's QuickBooks report says roughly 7 in 10 small and midsize businesses use AI regularly. Intuit surveyed about 34,000 owners across four countries, and its own materials are promotional"
  - "The Census Bureau changed its question wording last November, so comparisons across that date are imperfect. Firms' own forecasts of growth also shrank from 4.7 to 2.8 percentage points between January and July"
whyItMatters: "When you read that most businesses are 'using AI,' it matters who was asked. Surveys of people who already use a software company's tools skew toward early adopters. A government survey of all kinds of businesses, including plumbers and restaurants, shows a much slower spread. Both can be true, and the real story is that AI use is growing but is still far from universal."
consultingAngle: "If you are not using AI yet, you are not as far behind as headlines suggest, but the gap with large firms is real. Pick one routine task, such as drafting customer replies, summarizing meetings or sorting invoices, and test a tool on it for 30 days. If you already use AI, you may be ahead of your local competitors, which is a good moment to document what works. Be cautious with any vendor statistic in a sales pitch, and ask how many businesses were surveyed and who they were."
