It can read your local files, work with your email, Google Drive, Jira and Confluence, and run scheduled tasks, and Proton says it never trains on your chats and cannot read them."

keyDetails:
  - "What it is: an AI assistant from Proton, the Swiss company behind Proton Mail and Proton VPN, built as a privacy-first alternative to ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude"
  - "Who it's for: small businesses, professionals and anyone who handles sensitive information, such as client files, health details or finances, and wants AI help without handing that data to a big tech platform"
  - "What it does: connects to your local files, your browser, Proton Mail (through Proton Mail Bridge), Google Drive, Jira, Confluence and other tools that support the MCP standard, and can carry out tasks with them"
  - "Scheduled tasks: describe a job like 'every Monday, summarize emails that need a reply' and Lumo sets it up and runs it on schedule. Each task has its own permissions and can be paused or deleted"
  - "Skills: save instructions, templates and reference files, such as your writing style or a proposal template, and Lumo reuses them in every conversation"
  - "Privacy claims: Proton says it keeps no server-side logs, encrypts chat history so only you can read it, never uses conversations or connected files to train AI or target ads, and hosts data in Europe under Swiss privacy law"
  - "Important setting: Proton's own guide says every action is set to auto-approve by default when you add a connector. Change risky actions like sending or deleting email to 'Ask' or 'Deny'"
  - "Pricing: the desktop app requires a Proton account, and reports say it is aimed at Proton subscribers. Lumo Plus has been listed at $12.99 a month and business plans exist, but check Proton's site for current prices"
whyItMatters: "Most AI assistants are run by companies that make money from data, and today's news that Google will hold 'temporary' Gemini chats for two months is a reminder of how much they keep. Lumo is one of the few mainstream options built around not keeping your information. Now that it can work directly with your files and inbox, it is a practical choice for people who want AI help but have avoided it over privacy concerns."
consultingAngle: "Example: a bookkeeper or insurance agent with a folder of client receipts and a busy inbox. Each month they spend about three hours sorting bills, flagging unusual charges and writing a summary for each client. With Lumo Desktop, they set up one scheduled task to review the receipts folder on the first of every month, summarize spending and flag anything unusual, plus a weekly inbox summary of emails needing a reply, with drafts written in their saved style. The review drops to about 30 minutes of checking, and client data never goes to an ad-funded AI company. Before you start, open the settings for each connector and set sending, deleting and anything involving money to 'Ask', since everything is auto-approved out of the box. Proton has not published independent security test results, so if you work in a regulated field, ask Proton for its compliance documents before putting client data through it."
